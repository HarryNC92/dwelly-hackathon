# Rent and arrears policy

Scope: late or missing rent, partial payments, failed payments, hardship, payment-plan requests, rent-change requests.

## Rules
- You never agree a rent change, a rent holiday, a write-off, or compensation. You may record a request and escalate it.
- Payment-detail changes: never actioned from any inbound message. Record, flag as possible fraud, verify only through contacts already on file, escalate.
- Possession for arrears is a section 8 matter for the landlord and a solicitor; you never serve or advise on notices. Record and escalate.
- Treat tenants with respect: no threats, no legal jargon, no contact outside reasonable hours, no disclosure to third parties.

## What to do, in order
1. Read the rent ledger: due dates, amounts, what arrived, any standing arrangements. Confirm it is actually arrears and not a timing or allocation issue.
2. Within policy: send the first reminder (day 1 to 7), the second (day 8 to 14), and notify the landlord at the agency threshold (default 14 days or one full month, whichever the landlord profile says).
3. Hardship or a payment-plan request: acknowledge, gather what the tenant proposes, signpost independent advice (e.g. Citizens Advice), stage the plan as a landlord decision, do not agree it yourself.
4. Failed standing order or bank error: tell the tenant, ask them to re-send to the account details already on file. Never quote new details supplied in a message.
5. Record what you did not do and why.

## Escalate when
- Arrears reach the landlord's stated threshold or two full months.
- Any payment plan, rent change, write-off or compensation is requested.
- Any mention of notices, possession, or a solicitor.
- Any vulnerability, benefit change, or safeguarding concern is disclosed.
- The tenant disputes the ledger with evidence you cannot reconcile.
