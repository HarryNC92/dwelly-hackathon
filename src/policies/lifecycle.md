# Tenancy lifecycle policy

Scope: enquiries, viewings, applications and referencing, offers, move-in, renewals, tenant notice, landlord requests for possession, check-out and deposits.

## Rules (Renters' Rights Act regime, England, from 1 May 2026)
- Tenancies are periodic. There is no fixed term to renew; a "renewal" is a confirmation, not a new agreement.
- Tenant notice to leave: two months, in writing. Record it, confirm the end date, start the check-out process.
- Landlord wanting the property back: only on section 8 grounds (for example selling, moving in, serious arrears, antisocial behaviour) with the statutory notice period. You never serve, draft or advise on a notice. Record the request and escalate.
- Rent increases: section 13 only, at most once a year, at least two months' notice, tenant may challenge. Record and escalate; never implement.
- Applicants: no blanket refusal for benefits, children, or pets (pet requests must be considered reasonably). Referencing is on affordability and history, applied consistently. Record the criteria used.
- Before a move-in: valid gas safety record, EICR, EPC, deposit protection plan, Right to Rent check, How to Rent guide. A missing item blocks the move-in date in your resolution.
- Deposits: deductions only for evidenced damage beyond fair wear and tear, cleaning to the inventory standard, or unpaid rent. Propose the figures with evidence references; the deposit scheme and the tenant decide the rest.

## What to do
1. Enquiry or viewing: check availability and criteria, book the viewing within the agency's slots, tell the applicant what to bring.
2. Application: run through referencing as the policy states; record the decision factors.
3. Move-in: build the checklist against the compliance register; stage the inventory and key handover; block if anything is missing.
4. Notice from tenant: acknowledge, confirm dates, schedule check-out and inventory, tell the landlord.
5. Check-out: compare inventory, propose deductions with evidence, send the proposal.

## Escalate when
- Any possession, notice, or rent-change request.
- A discrimination risk in an instruction (e.g. "no families").
- Deposit deductions are disputed or exceed the evidence.
- The move-in is blocked by a compliance gap the landlord will not fix.
