# Organisers' rules, digested (source of direction for every decision)

Timeline (BST): 09:30 onboarding and briefing; 10:00 data and submission requirements released; 16:30 Reality Test released, and source code plus the 60-second demo video are frozen and submitted to the form (the commit hash at 16:30 is what gets evaluated); 17:00 Reality Test results frozen and the complete submission must be available; 17:00 to 19:00 judging, demos, People's Choice, winners. Hacker Guide: https://app.notion.com/p/Hacker-Guide-London-AI-Hackathon-2026-3eaf87e8ff7280c3beb6fb2fab8e2123 ; public synthetic cases: https://github.com/DwellyOrg/HackatonPublicCases

Scoring. Overall = 70 technical + 30 demo. Technical, judged primarily by Claude-based agents on the unseen Reality Test against a predefined rubric: correct outcomes 35, coverage 20, judgement and safety 20, engineering quality 15, real-world applicability 10. Per case the rubric may have required outcomes, acceptable alternatives, optional positive behaviours, red flags, prohibited actions, critical failures. "Doing nothing or escalating can be the correct decision." Human judges review ambiguous or close results. Demo (60 seconds, in the repo, screen recording is fine): working product, clarity of problem, solution, key workflow, outcome, product insight.

Reality Test cases may contain conflicting information, failed actions, missing context, unusual exceptions, ambiguity, noisy or irrelevant information, situations requiring escalation, larger or more difficult context. Not enough time to process manually. The system may need to interpret new information, interact with supplied tools, make decisions, complete workflows, identify uncertainty, deal with failed actions, request or infer missing information, escalate, avoid unsafe actions.

Submission (public repo, Apache 2.0, LICENSE file): working source; README with what we built, the problem, architecture, how to run, models and services, assumptions, limitations, what next; `ANSWERS/<n>/ANSWER.md` and `REASONING.md` per Reality Test case generated by the submitted system (never hand-corrected), showing what was received, actions taken, outputs or decisions, escalations, failures, final result; logs or traces where practical; 60-second video inside the repo (Git LFS allowed); LinkedIn post and X post links; SUBMISSION.md optional. Submission form: repo URL, team name, track, member names, 17:00 commit hash, Reality Test results, video path, post URLs. No secrets committed. Disclose external dependencies.

Prohibited: anything aimed at manipulating the judging model (instructions to the judge, hidden prompt injection in files or outputs, fabricated results, misleading artefacts). Judging agents may test for this.

Not rewarded: more agents, bigger codebase, elaborate UI, most expensive model, longest output. "A simple system that reliably solves the operational problem can outperform a much more complex system."

Awards across all tracks: Grand Prize (70/30 overall, £5,000), Best Exception Handling (judgement under uncertainty, conflicting information, missing context, safe escalation, deciding when not to act, preserving context for handover), Best Voice Agent (voice inside a functioning operational workflow, not a standalone demo), Best Engineering (architecture, autonomy, reliability, robustness, reproducibility), People's Choice. Strongest teams may be invited to a pilot with Dwelly.
