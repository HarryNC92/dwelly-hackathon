# Compliance policy

Scope: statutory certificates, inspections, licensing, safety duties, information duties.

## Duties (England) and what counts as compliant
- Gas safety: a Gas Safe engineer checks every gas appliance and flue every 12 months. The record goes to existing tenants within 28 days and to new tenants before they move in. No valid record means no new tenancy starts and existing tenants are at risk: book immediately.
- Electrical safety: an EICR every 5 years by a qualified person; remedial work from the report within 28 days (or sooner if the report says so); report to the tenant within 28 days of inspection.
- Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms: a smoke alarm on every storey with living accommodation; a CO alarm in any room with a fixed combustion appliance (gas cookers excepted). Repair or replace when told one is faulty.
- EPC: a property must have a valid EPC of band E or better to be let (minimum energy efficiency standard). Treat stricter future targets as not yet in force unless the briefing says otherwise.
- Deposits: protected in a government scheme within 30 days of receipt, with the prescribed information served in the same window. Deposit capped at five weeks' rent (annual rent under £50,000).
- Right to Rent: checked before the tenancy starts, with follow-up checks where the right is time-limited.
- Renters' Rights Act 2025 (in force from 1 May 2026): tenancies are periodic; section 21 no longer exists; possession only on section 8 grounds; rent rises only by section 13 notice, at most once a year, with at least two months' notice and a tenant right to challenge at the tribunal; no blanket bans on pets, children or benefits; landlords must register on the private rented sector database and join the ombudsman as the scheme rolls out. If anything in the case contradicts this (for example an instruction to serve a section 21), do not act, record it, escalate.
- Damp and mould: treat as a hazard with investigation and remediation timescales; do not blame lifestyle.
- Licensing: HMO licensing for 5 or more people in 2 or more households; local selective or additional licensing may apply. An unlicensed property that needs a licence is an escalation, not a form to fill.
- Data: share only what each party needs (UK GDPR).

## What to do, in order
1. Read the certificate register for the property, the tenancy dates, and any open compliance tickets.
2. Compute status against the case date ("now"), never the wall clock: valid, expiring within 30 days, expired, missing.
3. Expiring or expired: book the inspection with an approved contractor by the required date, tell the tenant the access window, tell the landlord. Missing on a property with a new tenancy pending: block the move-in in the resolution and escalate.
4. Tenant refusing access for a safety check: record every attempt, write the formal access request, escalate if a second attempt fails. Never threaten.
5. Landlord refusing or ignoring: record the instruction, state the legal position in the escalation, do not drop the duty.

## Escalate when
- A duty cannot be met by the deadline with the tools available.
- The landlord instructs you to let, renew or increase rent on a non-compliant property.
- Any instruction involving notices, eviction, deposit deductions beyond the evidenced amount, or a rent increase.
- Licensing status is unclear or a licence is required and absent.
- A certificate document is unreadable, inconsistent with the register, or looks altered.
