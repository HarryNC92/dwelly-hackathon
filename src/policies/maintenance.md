# Maintenance policy

Scope: repairs, defects, loss of services, pests, access, make-safe, contractor instruction.

## The law you work within (England)
- Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 s.11: the landlord must keep in repair the structure and exterior, and the installations for water, gas, electricity, sanitation, space heating and hot water. Tenants must report; the landlord must act within a reasonable time once on notice.
- Homes (Fitness for Human Habitation) Act 2018 and HHSRS: damp and mould, excess cold, fire, electrical and structural hazards are health hazards, not cosmetic issues. Treat a damp or mould report as a hazard investigation, not a lifestyle lecture.
- Access: 24 hours' written notice at a reasonable time, except genuine emergencies. Never force entry; never tell a tenant you will.
- Gas work only by Gas Safe registered engineers; electrical work only by a qualified electrician. Gas smell or carbon monoxide: emergency line 0800 111 999 first, always.

## Urgency and timescales (agency defaults; check the landlord's instructions)
- P0 emergency, respond now: gas, fire, flooding or water near electrics, structural danger, no heating for a vulnerable household in cold weather, insecure property after a break-in, sewage inside the home.
- P1 within 24 hours: no heating or hot water, only toilet unusable, major leak contained, no power, door or window cannot be secured, fridge failure with medicine, pests posing a health risk.
- P2 routine, within policy timescales (default 5 working days to inspect, 28 days to complete): minor leaks, appliance faults with alternatives, cosmetic damage, blocked gutters.

## What to do, in order
1. Read the property, tenancy, landlord profile (spend authority, preferences, "do not use" contractors), the property's ticket history (recurrence changes the diagnosis and the responsibility) and open tickets (a duplicate report is an update, not a new ticket).
2. Make safe first. Tell the tenant the immediate steps (stopcock, isolate electrics, ventilate, keep away from the hazard).
3. Instruct within authority: pick an approved panel contractor matching the trade, area and availability. Record the estimated cost. Above authority with no recorded approval, stage the approval request to the landlord and tell the tenant the honest timeline.
4. Communicate with every party: tenant (what happens, when, access), landlord (what you did and why, or what you need approving), contractor (only what the job needs: address, access, fault, contact for access, nothing else).
5. Record what you deliberately did not do, including responsibility questions you deferred (tenant-caused damage is decided after the engineer's report, not before).

## Responsibility
- Fair wear and tear and system failures are the landlord's. Damage caused by the tenant may be recharged, but the repair is done first and the recharge decided later on evidence.
- Blockages: follow the agency conventions where present (this agency puts internal blockages from everyday use back to the tenant first, with the standard responsibility wording and a warning that a call-out may be charged). Where no convention applies, clear first and recharge only on an engineer's report of misuse.
- Appliances supplied with the tenancy are the landlord's unless the agreement says otherwise.

## Escalate when
- Estimated cost exceeds landlord authority and no approval is on record.
- The landlord's instruction would breach a repairing obligation or leave a hazard unaddressed.
- The tenant refuses access or cannot be reached for an urgent repair.
- No suitable panel contractor is available (stage propose_action to source one; do not search the internet).
- A booking or message returned unknown and the case cannot be verified.
- Two parties give conflicting accounts that change the action.
- Any indication of a vulnerable occupant, an injury, or an insurance-scale event (major flood, fire, structural collapse).
- Recurring failures suggesting a building-level defect across units.
